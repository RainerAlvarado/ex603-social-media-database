# Unit 4 Finding: The actual failure rate, and where it concentrates

**To:** Leadership team
**From:** Trust and Data
**Re:** Follow-up to the spam-removal correction: what the real rate is and which posts to look at

## The platform-wide rate

Across all 200 reactions in the data, 12 were removed as spam. That is a failure rate of 6 percent, the same figure the earlier audit arrived at, now confirmed by counting failures and totals together in one pass rather than separately. For context, another 22 reactions were removed for other reasons (duplicates, policy violations, users withdrawing their own reaction) and 10 are flagged and still under review. The 6 percent counts only spam.

The rate is not uniform. By region of origin, India is highest at 4 spam removals in 40 reactions (10 percent), then Brazil and the US at 3 in roughly 40 each (about 8 percent), with the UK and Vietnam at 1 each (around 2.5 percent). The five regions add up to the 200 total exactly, so nothing was dropped on the way to the summary.

## How it varies across posts

Thirteen posts received reactions. Their spam rates range from zero to just under 19 percent, but most of that spread is explained by volume. A post with six reactions and one spam removal shows a 17 percent rate, and that number says more about the sample size than the post. So before comparing, we set a floor: only posts with at least 10 reactions get a rate, and for the "high-volume" comparison we used posts at or above the platform average of about 15 reactions each.

Among those eight high-volume posts, the pooled spam rate is 4.8 percent. Seven of the eight sit between zero and 6.3 percent, which is the normal band. One does not.

## Which posts to investigate

**Post_02** is the clear outlier. 16 reactions, 3 removed as spam, an 18.8 percent rate, which is 3.9 times the high-volume baseline and three times the platform rate. No other post with comparable volume comes close. One of its three spam removals came from a user who also has a spam removal on Post_01, so this may be partly about who is reacting and not only about the post itself.

**Post_03** is second. 13 reactions, 2 removed as spam, a 15.4 percent rate. It also carries 2 flagged reactions still under review, one of its spam removals came from a user who has another on Post_11, and the post itself is currently hidden from the platform. A hidden post that is still attracting reactions, with nearly a third of them either spam or under review, is worth understanding.

**Post_04** shows a 16.7 percent rate but on only 6 reactions, one of which was spam. We would not open an investigation on one event. We would watch it: if its volume grows and the rate holds, it moves onto the list.

## One thing the averages were hiding

While building the baseline we found that every reaction removed from the platform has its viewing time recorded as zero. That is a side effect of removal, not a measurement, but it means any "average time on post" figure that includes removed reactions is pulled down by about 17 percent. The dwell-time numbers in the current dashboard should be recomputed over counted reactions only before they are used for anything.

## Recommendation

Open a targeted review of Post_02 and Post_03 by identifier, starting with the small set of users responsible for the spam removals on each. Leave the platform-wide rate at 6 percent in the dashboard. Add a minimum-volume rule to any per-post rate shown to leadership so that a single event on a low-traffic post cannot put it at the top of the list.
